**Primary conclusion:** the investigations do **not** establish a Universal Pattern Engine in the strong sense of one universal semantic IR, one generalized runtime, one notion of equivalence, one similarity/confidence scale, or one discovery procedure. They do, however, establish a surprisingly coherent **bounded common semantic architecture** above those implementation layers. The evidence best supports:

> **a shared declarative symbolic-pattern envelope and result/evidence contract, classified at compile time into multiple specialized execution models, with discovery as a separate bounded search architecture that produces and replays the same declarative pattern representation used for matching.**

That is closest to the permitted outcome **“shared representation with specialized runtimes,” augmented by “shared matching language but separate discovery architecture.”** It is stronger than mere loose interoperability, but substantially narrower than a universal engine. ### Genuine common abstractions

The **observation/projection distinction** is genuine. The attribute report argues that an observation is an occurrence-specific anchor and that interpretations belong in typed, context-indexed projections rather than in an ever-growing feature bag. The linguistic report independently reaches the same shape because code points, graphemes, lexemes, phonemes, syllables, and prosodic structures have non-aligned boundaries and many-to-many mappings. The equivalence report arrives at a compatible conclusion from another direction: normalization, confusable skeletonization, transliteration, and contextual interpretation must be explicit operations whose provenance survives matching. fileciteturn0file7 fileciteturn0file5 fileciteturn0file6

The **typed-relation distinction** is also genuine. The relational-algebra report explicitly separates pattern structure from propositions about values and recommends closed, type-aware relational theories; the attribute model independently says n-ary relations should remain relations rather than being flattened into feature columns. The equivalence investigation reinforces this by showing that equality, canonical equivalence, confusability, similarity, transduction, and uncertain interpretation have different formal properties. fileciteturn0file4 fileciteturn0file7 fileciteturn0file6

The **exact-versus-approximate boundary** is unusually well supported. Weighted similarity says a distance, cost, score, fuzzy degree, probability, and calibrated probability are different mathematical objects. Meaningfulness says compression, significance, prediction, stability, novelty, usefulness, causality, and semantic meaning are likewise different kinds of evidence. Equivalence says confusability or approximate interpretation does not become equivalence merely because a threshold is crossed. Linguistics says provider uncertainty and pronunciation alternatives must remain visible instead of turning into generic fuzzy truth. fileciteturn0file3 fileciteturn0file0 fileciteturn0file6 fileciteturn0file5

Finally, **witness structures are more strongly justified than any particular runtime abstraction**. Depending on the domain, the witness may contain source spans, a projection chain, an equivalence profile, an interleaving schedule, capture values, relation parameters, an alignment, raw evidence components, discovery provenance, statistical assumptions, or computational-plan information. The common abstraction is therefore not one fixed witness schema with identical fields; it is the requirement that successful results carry a typed derivation that can be inspected back to source observations and the semantic rules that licensed the result. fileciteturn0file4 fileciteturn0file5 fileciteturn0file6 fileciteturn0file3 fileciteturn0file2

### Similar concepts that should remain separate

Several repeated words conceal different abstractions.

**Projection** has at least four meanings across the investigations. The attribute and linguistic reports use projection to mean a typed interpretation of source material; the relational report uses projection to define an ordered structural view or lane; the equivalence report includes deterministic transforms such as normalization and transliteration; the weighted report discusses normalization of numerical evidence before aggregation. Those can share an explicit-stage convention and provenance model, but treating them as one polymorphic `Projection` operation without semantic subtypes would erase important guarantees such as reversibility, multiplicity, lossiness, and context dependence. fileciteturn0file7 fileciteturn0file5 fileciteturn0file4 fileciteturn0file6 fileciteturn0file3

**Equivalence** is even more dangerous. Unicode canonical equivalence and an explicitly declared equivalence class have reflexive, symmetric, transitive semantics. UTS-style confusability, phonetic resemblance, an edit-distance threshold, a discovery engine's “observationally equivalent on this data” pruning relation, and zero distance under a particular pseudometric can all produce superficially similar grouping behavior without making the same claim. They should retain separate relation identities and separate witnesses. fileciteturn0file6 fileciteturn0file5 fileciteturn0file3 fileciteturn0file2

**Weight** and **score** also recur analogically rather than semantically. A weighted automaton's semiring weight, a visual-similarity component, a user-authored decision weight, a learned model parameter, a discovery search priority, an MDL codelength, and a statistical p-value or posterior do not have a common algebra merely because they are numeric. This is one of the clearest reasons a single generic `confidence: float` result would be architecturally unsound. fileciteturn0file3 fileciteturn0file0 fileciteturn0file2

Likewise, **ambiguity** must be typed. Competing linguistic analyses are epistemic alternatives about interpretation; nondeterministic automaton states are an execution technique; several matching parses may represent semantic multiplicity; discovery candidates are hypotheses; statistical uncertainty is uncertainty about an estimate or event. A common alternative/choice container may be useful, but the quantification and result semantics cannot be inherited from one case to another. fileciteturn0file5 fileciteturn0file1 fileciteturn0file2 fileciteturn0file0

## Architecture synthesis

### A shared semantic envelope is justified; one universal lower-level IR is not

The evidence supports a **common semantic envelope** whose job is to state what a pattern means before deciding how it executes. It plausibly needs typed subjects/views, structural composition, captures, explicitly named projections/transforms, typed relations, ambiguity/alternative semantics, exact versus approximate result kinds, and enough metadata to produce witnesses and classify computational requirements. This conclusion is synthesized from independent convergence in the attribute, relational, equivalence, linguistic, and complexity investigations. fileciteturn0file7 fileciteturn0file4 fileciteturn0file6 fileciteturn0file5 fileciteturn0file1

That does **not** establish one lower-level Semantic IR. The attribute investigation explicitly cautions that its typed observation/projection model is a research vocabulary rather than a STRling IR and says reusable representational concepts do not establish a universal architecture. fileciteturn0file7 The relational report points toward semantic classification into regular/symbolic, scalar-relational, sequence-relational, or theory-specific representations rather than continuously expanding a regex IR. fileciteturn0file4 The linguistic report recommends a general structural layer plus specialized language technology, often represented as graphs/lattices and implemented with different providers or finite-state relations where appropriate. fileciteturn0file5

The complexity report is the one source that sketches a “Canonical Semantic IR” before dispatch, but its actual computational finding is that semantic dependency changes the execution model and that finite-state, parser/DP, relational, CSP/SAT/SMT, and other backends require different guarantees. fileciteturn0file1 Cross-report reconciliation therefore suggests interpreting its “Canonical Semantic IR” as a **canonical semantic boundary or typed AST**, not as evidence that all semantics should lower into one homogeneous executable IR.

**Judgment on one Semantic IR:** **not justified at present.** A shared semantic AST, serialization, or contract is justified more strongly than a shared lower-level IR. Family-specific IRs behind an explicit classification boundary are better supported.

### One pattern language with multiple execution backends is conditionally justified

A **bounded declarative pattern language** is more defensible than a universal runtime. The relational investigation shows that a regular structural spine can coexist with typed closed relations while preserving declarative intent and preventing arbitrary computation. fileciteturn0file4 The complexity report demonstrates that those semantics can then be dispatched to very different computational models rather than pretending every construct is finite-state. fileciteturn0file1 Discovery independently benefits from an explicit hypothesis language because search is tractable only when operators, types, parameters, depth, recursion, approximation, and resources are bounded. fileciteturn0file2

The qualification is important. “One pattern language” should not mean one monolithic namespace containing every linguistic model, statistical test, solver theory, graphical relation, and discovery heuristic. Linguistic interpretation requires specialized, profile-scoped resources; weighted evidence aggregation needs explicitly named mathematical contracts; meaningfulness evaluation requires null models, perturbations, task utilities, and other evidence machinery that is not itself pattern truth. fileciteturn0file5 fileciteturn0file3 fileciteturn0file0

A more defensible formulation is therefore:

> **One typed declarative pattern envelope with bounded extension domains and multiple execution backends is justified as an architecture hypothesis; one closed universal language containing all domain machinery is not.**

This resembles an extensible semantic language or typed dialect system more than a single generalized regex.

### Relational pattern algebra is a bounded common kernel, not the whole architecture

The relational report supplies the clearest candidate kernel: familiar regular composition for arrangement, explicit ordered views and interleaving, immutable captures, and closed relations lifted over adjacent elements, windows, and correspondences. It deliberately excludes arbitrary callbacks, arbitrary recursion, mutable state, unrestricted folds, and captured values constructing future patterns. fileciteturn0file4

Other reports support pieces of that model without establishing that all domains reduce to it. Linguistic alignments and many-to-many mappings fit explicit views and correspondences well, while equivalence/transduction supplies additional relation families. fileciteturn0file5 fileciteturn0file6 The complexity report, however, shows that recursion, parsing, graph structure, unrestricted equality joins, and other classes cross important computational boundaries. fileciteturn0file1

The proper conclusion is therefore **a relational algebra is a strong bounded substrate for sequence and structured-value patterns, not a proof that every symbolic pattern domain shares one algebraic runtime**.

### Matching and discovery should share the final pattern representation, not the engine

This is one of the clearest positive findings. The discovery report recommends bounded search over an externally defined declarative algebra, specialized proposal mechanisms, and independent replay of surviving candidates through the ordinary matcher. Search may use sequence mining, symbolic regression, ILP, program synthesis, constraint solving, multi-model fitting, or learned guidance internally, but the accepted output should serialize as an ordinary pattern whose semantics do not depend on the search process that found it. fileciteturn0file2

The complexity report independently establishes why discovery needs a separate planner: a pattern language can be cheap to match and hard to infer. A fixed finite-state matcher can be input-linear even where finding an appropriate automaton or pattern from observations is combinatorial. fileciteturn0file1 The meaningfulness report adds another separation: discovering a true pattern is not the same as establishing that it is statistically surprising, predictive, stable, novel, useful, causal, or semantically meaningful. fileciteturn0file0

Accordingly:

**Matching and discovery should share:**

the public/declarative candidate representation, its type rules, semantic evaluator, computational classifier where useful, and witness vocabulary. fileciteturn0file2 fileciteturn0file1

**They should not share by necessity:**

search-state representations, proposal algorithms, beam/branch-and-bound structures, residual models, statistical evaluation state, training models, or resource planners. fileciteturn0file2 fileciteturn0file0

This gives a precise answer to placement: **discovery belongs beside the STRling matching/compiler core, not inside its ordinary matching runtime and not entirely outside its semantic ecosystem.** Architecturally it is an adjacent consumer/producer of the same pattern representation and a client of the matcher as verification authority. Whether that adjacent subsystem belongs in the same product, package, repository, or organization is a downstream product decision not established by these investigations. fileciteturn0file2 fileciteturn6file0L1-L7

### The architecture is centralized where semantics matter and interoperable where algorithms differ

A purely centralized architecture would require domain projection, matching, approximate evidence, discovery, evaluation, and execution to conform to one engine. The reports repeatedly show why that is a poor boundary: phonology needs specialized analyzers and lexicons; relational patterns change execution class based on dependency structure; weighting requires model-specific algebras; discovery requires specialized search algorithms; statistical meaningfulness requires domain-specific nulls and perturbations. fileciteturn0file5 fileciteturn0file1 fileciteturn0file3 fileciteturn0file2 fileciteturn0file0

A completely federated collection of unrelated systems would lose real convergence: it would duplicate pattern representation, erase a common match/discovery verification boundary, and make witness/provenance interoperability harder despite those being recurrent needs. fileciteturn0file7 fileciteturn0file4 fileciteturn0file2

The evidence therefore favors a middle position:

| Centralize or standardize | Keep specialized / interoperable |
|---|---|
| Observation/source identity and extent conventions | Linguistic analyzers, lexicons, G2P, syllabification, prosody |
| Typed representation/projection contracts | Domain projection implementations |
| Declarative pattern semantics and serialization | Finite-state, parser, DP, relational, solver, graph runtimes |
| Relation identities and exact/approximate result kinds | Similarity metrics and statistical models |
| Capture/witness/provenance conventions | Backend-specific optimization and indexes |
| Computational-class metadata and resource contracts | Backend execution planners |
| Accepted discovery-candidate representation | Candidate proposal/search algorithms |
| Replay/verification protocol | Search heuristics and learned guidance |
| Evidence record types | Null models, perturbations, utilities and causal models |

That architecture is **interoperability through semantic contracts rather than unification through shared implementation**. fileciteturn0file7 fileciteturn0file1 fileciteturn0file2

## Contradictions, rejected unifications, and missing evidence

### Material tensions among the completed investigations

There are few outright factual contradictions among the eight reports. The important conflicts are architectural assumptions at their boundaries.

**The largest tension is “one canonical Semantic IR” versus “shared semantics do not justify one IR.”** The complexity report uses a canonical Semantic IR in its dispatch sketch, while the attribute report explicitly refuses to elevate its common representation into a STRling IR and the relational report proposes semantic classification into different IR families. fileciteturn0file1 fileciteturn0file7 fileciteturn0file4 This synthesis resolves the tension conservatively: the evidence justifies a **canonical semantic contract or AST boundary**, but not one executable lower-level IR. The canonical-IR assumption remains unproven.

**A second tension is the relational report's deliberately regular structural core versus the complexity report's broader catalogue of recursive, context-free, tree, graph, and solver-backed semantics.** fileciteturn0file4 fileciteturn0file1 This is not best resolved by expanding the relational core until it covers everything. The relational algebra should instead be read as one bounded, analyzable family within a larger dispatchable semantic envelope. More expressive families need explicit classification rather than silent admission into the same guarantees.

**A third tension concerns “ordinary matcher verification” in discovery.** The discovery report's cleanest invariant is that a discovered candidate should serialize and replay through ordinary matching semantics. fileciteturn0file2 But linguistic and approximate domains can depend on versioned external resources, alternative analyses, learned models, or provider-native probabilities. fileciteturn0file5 fileciteturn0file3 Replay is therefore only reproducible if those dependencies are part of the pattern/profile/environment contract. A pattern referencing “phonetic similarity under provider X/version Y/model Z” may still be declarative, but the matcher cannot pretend that provider output is intrinsic deterministic data.

**A fourth tension concerns scalar search objectives.** Discovery practically needs heuristic ranking or cost functions, while meaningfulness and weighted-similarity research strongly reject treating one score as intrinsic truth. fileciteturn0file2 fileciteturn0file0 fileciteturn0file3 The reconciliation is straightforward but important: a search priority may be a private operational heuristic; it must not be promoted into the candidate's semantic “confidence” or final meaningfulness.

### Unification attempts the evidence now rejects

The reports collectively reject several tempting architectures.

**Universal feature bag.** The attribute report finds that observations should anchor typed projections and claims, not accumulate one universal collection of properties; linguistics provides a concrete counterexample because character, grapheme, phoneme, syllable, morphology, and prosody do not share one segmentation. fileciteturn0file7 fileciteturn0file5

**Universal equivalence or normalization relation.** Canonical equivalence, compatibility folding, confusability, transliteration, OCR interpretation, homophony, and approximate resemblance make different claims and compose differently. fileciteturn0file6 fileciteturn0file5

**Universal fuzzy/confidence scalar.** Weighted similarity demonstrates that numeric values belong to different mathematical types; meaningfulness demonstrates that even higher-level evidence should remain multidimensional and often non-compensatory. fileciteturn0file3 fileciteturn0file0 This directly contradicts the historical UPE proposal's generic confidence-oriented direction. fileciteturn3file0L1-L7

**Universal execution VM.** The computational investigation establishes qualitatively different models and complexity boundaries for regular recognition, finite-state transduction, parsing, relational joins, bounded-window processing, tree/graph evaluation, CSP/SAT/SMT, and unrestricted callbacks. fileciteturn0file1 A generic VM could exist as an implementation wrapper, but the reports provide no evidence that doing so would yield the best semantics or guarantees.

**Universal discovery algorithm.** Discovery prior art converges on specialized proposal strategies within bounded hypothesis spaces, not one algorithm that efficiently searches all symbolic structures. fileciteturn0file2

**Universal meaningfulness function.** Compression, significance, held-out prediction, stability, novelty, task usefulness, causality, and semantic meaning answer different questions. The meaningfulness report recommends validity gates and evidence profiles rather than one domain-independent score. fileciteturn0file0

**Universal lower-level IR.** The cross-report evidence is too heterogeneous to justify it. A shared semantic representation remains plausible, but the report set contains direct warnings that common vocabulary and reusable structural concepts do not imply one IR. fileciteturn0file7 fileciteturn0file4 fileciteturn0file5

### Evidence still missing

The common architecture is coherent enough to become a better research hypothesis, but several load-bearing claims remain unvalidated.

**A cross-domain semantic prototype is missing.** None of the reports demonstrates one actual declarative representation expressing representative cases from, for example, exact symbolic equivalence, linguistic projection, relational sequence matching, and approximate matching while preserving each domain's semantics. Until such a prototype exists, “shared language” remains an inference from conceptual convergence rather than demonstrated interoperability. fileciteturn0file7 fileciteturn0file5 fileciteturn0file4 fileciteturn0file3

**Formal composition semantics are incomplete.** The reports separately analyze projection, equivalence, captures, relational constraints, alternatives, and approximation, but do not provide one formal account proving which combinations preserve determinism, equivalence laws, finite-state execution, or decidability. The complexity report makes clear that apparently modest operator compositions can move between major complexity classes. fileciteturn0file1

**The proposed semantic-versus-IR boundary has not been experimentally tested.** It remains unknown whether a shared AST plus specialized IRs is measurably simpler, more analyzable, or more maintainable than multiple specialized DSLs joined only at result and provenance boundaries. The active UPE inquiry explicitly names integration cost and specialized-system comparison as unresolved evidence needs. fileciteturn4file0L1-L7

**Backend conformance evidence is missing.** A multi-backend language would need cross-backend tests showing that specialized runtimes preserve the same declared semantics, witnesses, capture behavior, approximation contracts, and failure modes wherever their domains overlap. The current reports establish theoretical execution boundaries rather than such conformance. fileciteturn0file1

**Discovery replay has not been validated at system scale.** The discovery architecture argues persuasively for proposal-versus-verification separation, but there is not yet benchmark evidence showing that useful candidate spaces can be searched under realistic bounds, that discovered candidates serialize without semantic loss, and that replay produces the same evidence independently of discovery state. fileciteturn0file2

**Meaningfulness evaluation lacks cross-domain validation.** The multidimensional framework is methodologically strong, but null models, dependence assumptions, perturbations, recurrence definitions, task utility, and semantic grounding necessarily remain domain-specific. fileciteturn0file0 This limits how much common discovery infrastructure can claim beyond producing evidence records.

**Provider trust and reproducibility need more work.** Linguistic and approximate matching can depend on external resources and learned models whose versions, calibration, dialect coverage, and failure behavior are semantically material. The linguistic and weighted reports require provenance but do not establish a shared provider lifecycle or reproducibility standard. fileciteturn0file5 fileciteturn0file3

**Most importantly, user/product evidence is absent.** The reports establish technical and semantic plausibility, not that developers benefit from learning one generalized symbolic language instead of using several specialized systems. The active UPE inquiry identifies common user jobs, economic value, and integration cost as unresolved. fileciteturn4file0L1-L7 That gap should prevent architectural convergence from being interpreted as product justification.

## Computational and discovery implications

The complexity investigation provides the strongest reason not to equate a shared declarative language with a shared runtime. Its central finding is that the execution boundary occurs when a pattern's semantic dependency structure changes computational model, not when syntax stops looking like regex. Symbolic predicates over large domains can remain finite-state, while substring equality, unrestricted joins, graph topology, recursion, or arbitrary callbacks can change matching from stream-linear into polynomial, parameterized, NP-hard, solver-dependent, or undecidable classes. fileciteturn0file1

That has a direct architectural implication: **computational classification should be a compiler-visible semantic property, not a hidden backend implementation detail.** A pattern should be able to acquire a computational signature describing the variables on which a guarantee depends, relevant structural bounds, memory class, compilation risk, execution class, and whether exact evaluation is rejected or budgeted. fileciteturn0file1 The relational report independently supports the same principle by insisting that non-regular power be classified instead of hidden behind innocuous-looking relational syntax. fileciteturn0file4

A plausible family of externally understandable classifications would distinguish at least fixed-pattern stream-linear execution, symbolic stream-linear execution with predicate-cost qualifications, bounded-depth or bounded-window execution, polynomial combined complexity, parameterized classes, output-sensitive execution, explicitly budgeted combinatorial search, solver-fragment execution, and unknown/rejected semantics. The exact taxonomy belongs to downstream design, but the research evidence strongly supports having **some** explicit taxonomy rather than one generic “supported” flag. fileciteturn0file1

This also sharpens the separation between matching and discovery:

\[
\operatorname{Match}(P,D)
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{Discover}(\mathcal H,D)
=
\text{search for }P\in\mathcal H
\]

are computationally different problems even where \(P\) comes from exactly the same declarative language. fileciteturn0file1 The discovery investigation consequently treats the hypothesis language and its bounds—operator vocabulary, structural size, constants, recursion, approximation, partition complexity, and resource limits—as part of the discovery problem definition. fileciteturn0file2

The correct relationship is therefore:

> **same semantic pattern representation, different computational problem and different planner.**

A matching planner asks which backend can execute a supplied pattern. A discovery planner asks how to search a bounded set of possible patterns and may use several proposal algorithms before verification. Sharing low-level matching kernels or computational analyses may be efficient, but planner identity should not be assumed. fileciteturn0file2 fileciteturn0file1

The evidence evaluator should be a third concern rather than part of either semantics engine. Discovery can find a perfectly true recurring candidate that is statistically unsurprising after accounting for the search process, redundant with an existing explanation, unstable under perturbation, or useless for a task. Conversely, a locally scoped but rare pattern may be important despite low global coverage. fileciteturn0file0 Thus a clean architecture distinguishes:

\[
\text{pattern truth}
\neq
\text{search priority}
\neq
\text{statistical evidence}
\neq
\text{decision utility}.
\]

The weighted-similarity report reaches the analogous conclusion inside approximate matching:

\[
\text{relation truth}
\neq
\text{distance}
\neq
\text{cost}
\neq
\text{ranking score}
\neq
\text{probability}.
\]

fileciteturn0file3

Together, those findings imply a useful invariant for any common pattern language:

> **The declarative pattern specifies the claim being tested; a backend specifies how that claim is evaluated; a witness explains the result; discovery specifies how candidates are proposed; and an evidence framework specifies why a discovered result deserves attention.**

No one of those layers should silently acquire the semantics of another. fileciteturn0file4 fileciteturn0file1 fileciteturn0file2 fileciteturn0file0

## Research Intelligence and STRling disposition

The eight reports provide enough evidence to reject the historical UPE hypothesis **in its strong form**.

This is an inference from the eight reports, not a proposed product name or roadmap. The active UPE inquiry explicitly requires a bounded evidence-backed convergence before reconsideration; this synthesis supplies a candidate for that narrower hypothesis while preserving the inquiry's warning that common vocabulary alone is insufficient. fileciteturn4file0L1-L7

### Recommendation for Research Intelligence

**Research Intelligence should retain these investigations within the existing Beyond Regex program rather than establish a dedicated Beyond Patterns program now.**

The reason is organizational as well as epistemic. The existing Beyond Regex program already defines its purpose as investigating generalized pattern systems, weighted and approximate matching, non-text sequences, AI-assisted synthesis, and boundaries among regular, recursive, probabilistic, and general-computation models. It explicitly asks which capabilities belong in shared systems and which require separate systems, and it names formal boundary maps, interoperable result/explanation models, and comparisons against specialized systems as future research opportunities. fileciteturn5file0L1-L7 The synthesis performed here is therefore squarely within that program's declared charter.

Creating a new “Beyond Patterns” program at this point would risk giving organizational permanence to exactly the architectural unification that the evidence still leaves conditional. The reports establish a bounded semantic convergence, but they do not yet establish a universal IR, implementation center, user category, or product boundary sufficiently distinct from Beyond Regex to warrant a new stable program. The repository itself defines programs as stable research areas rather than roadmap stages and emphasizes that exploratory conclusions do not become product commitments merely through organization. fileciteturn6file0L1-L7

The stronger research move is to treat this synthesis as a **cross-inquiry architecture finding inside Beyond Regex** and change the UPE question from:

> “Is there a Universal Pattern Engine?”

to the falsifiable bounded question:

> **“Can one typed declarative symbolic-pattern contract support materially different domains and execution classes with less semantic duplication and no loss of domain-specific correctness compared with interoperable specialized DSLs?”**

A future program split would become evidence-backed only after at least three things exist: a cross-domain prototype validating the semantic contract, conformance evidence across specialized runtimes including witnesses and complexity guarantees, and discovery experiments demonstrating candidate replay through the shared representation. Product/user evidence that this common language creates value beyond a collection of specialist tools would still be required before treating that convergence as a product thesis. These criteria follow directly from the current unresolved gaps rather than from the historical UPE roadmap. fileciteturn0file1 fileciteturn0file2 fileciteturn4file0L1-L7

### Non-normative downstream consideration for `strling`

Research Intelligence does not own STRling architecture or product decisions; the repository explicitly states that implementation-facing recommendations remain subject to acceptance or rejection by the downstream `strling` product authority. fileciteturn6file0L1-L7 Consequently, the following are **research findings warranting downstream consideration, not proposed roadmap items**.

The evidence is strong enough for `strling` product authority to separately consider whether future architecture should preserve room for **typed projection and relation semantics without forcing them into the existing regex IR**, whether successful matches should have an extensible **witness/provenance contract**, whether new semantic operators should carry **compiler-visible computational classifications and resource guarantees**, and whether any future discovery facility should consume and emit the **same stable declarative representation** used by ordinary matching while remaining operationally separate from the compiler/runtime. fileciteturn0file7 fileciteturn0file4 fileciteturn0file1 fileciteturn0file2

The evidence is **not** strong enough to recommend that `strling` adopt a universal IR, build a generalized VM, add generic confidence scores, incorporate linguistic analysis into the core, create a universal discovery engine, or treat Beyond Regex research as an implementation sequence. Those moves are contradicted or left unsupported by the completed investigations and by the active Research Intelligence authority boundary. fileciteturn0file3 fileciteturn0file5 fileciteturn0file1 fileciteturn6file0L1-L7

The net disposition is therefore:

> **The completed investigations falsify the strong Universal Pattern Engine hypothesis but support a narrower, coherent architecture: shared declarative symbolic semantics and explanation contracts, specialized execution backends, and a separate bounded-discovery system that returns patterns to the ordinary matcher. Research Intelligence should keep this work in Beyond Regex until cross-domain implementation and conformance evidence justifies a stronger organizational or architectural claim. Any resulting STRling architecture decision belongs to downstream product authority.** fileciteturn4file0L1-L7 fileciteturn5file0L1-L7 fileciteturn6file0L1-L7
