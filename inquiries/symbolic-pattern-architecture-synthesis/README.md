# Symbolic pattern architecture synthesis

## Research question

Where do the eight focused investigations genuinely support shared concepts for future symbolic-pattern research, where are their concepts only analogous or domain-specific, and does the resulting evidence justify any common architecture short of a universal engine?

## Motivation / decision informed

Only a cross-report synthesis should ask whether independently developed findings converge on a shared future architecture. The answer can guide formal research and prototype criteria without creating a STRling roadmap.

## Current state

All eight reports are now repository-integrated as provenance-bearing inputs with focused active inquiry syntheses. They are substantial AI-assisted investigations with partially verified source sets; their opaque research-session citation markers prevent a complete claim-level audit.

The [pre-input-gate assessment](reports/pre-input-gate-assessment.md) is preserved as superseded provenance, not final synthesis. When that earlier gate was run, the eight focused reports were not yet integrated and available to the repository process, so it could not establish an authoritative architectural conclusion. Its durable negative-knowledge lesson is that a synthesis must not manufacture convergence from missing or unauditable inputs. This README is a fresh synthesis of the now-integrated reports and does not adopt that assessment's architectural disposition.

## Scope

Typed observations and projections; context and provenance; equivalence and similarity; spans, groups, views, and relations; specialized linguistic projections; approximate evidence; declarative matching; bounded discovery and independent verification; meaningfulness profiles; computational classification; specialized execution backends; and architectural boundaries among them.

## Non-goals

This inquiry does not select one IR, runtime, syntax, provider model, database, solver, or product. It does not modify STRling, establish a roadmap, create Fourth Edition work, or infer downstream acceptance.

## Research method

The synthesis compares the eight active inquiry findings by ownership boundary and evidence path. A concept counts as shared only when more than repeated vocabulary supports it—for example, distinct domain requirements or independent prior-art lines lead to compatible constraints. Analogies, proposals that originate in one report and are merely reused elsewhere, and domain-specific internals are labeled separately.

## Evidence and source quality

The source reports draw on standards, formal-language theory, linguistic and statistical literature, database systems, pattern mining, and complexity results. Representative controlling sources were spot-checked during integration. Because the full claim sets and citations have not been independently audited and no cross-domain prototype exists, the synthesis is Exploratory rather than Evidence-backed or Recommendation-ready.

## Findings

### Shared concepts with support from several inquiries

| Concept | Independent support | Bounded synthesis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typed observations and multiple projections | Observation-model prior art distinguishes occurrence from interpretation; linguistic evidence requires several simultaneous, many-to-many analyses; equivalence work requires original spans to survive transformations. | A stable observation anchor plus typed projections is a credible shared semantic envelope. It is not yet a shared storage schema or IR. |
| Explicit context and provenance | Attribute claims depend on authority, derivation, and context; equivalence depends on profile, version, font, and direction; linguistic projections depend on language, dialect, lexicon, and provider; approximate evidence depends on model or policy provenance. | Context and provenance are semantic inputs to claims and results, not optional logging fields. Their exact representation may remain provider-specific. |
| Equivalence distinct from similarity and uncertainty | Unicode and security relations separate identity, normalization, and confusability; linguistic homophony and pronunciation alternatives separate relation from score; weighted evidence distinguishes distance, score, probability, and calibration. | Exact relation type must survive matching. Approximate evidence may qualify or rank a relation but cannot silently redefine equality. |
| Span, group, view, and correspondence structure | Composite observations require parts and extents; confusable substitutions can be many-to-many spans; linguistic layers require grouped units and alignments; relational patterns require explicit views, weave, and correspondence witnesses. | A future semantic contract needs more than scalar symbols. It must preserve source extents and explicit structural correspondence, while allowing domain-specific topology. |
| Declarative pattern intent separated from execution | Relational algebra seeks a finite typed AST; discovery requires candidates replayable as ordinary patterns; computational analysis requires semantic classification before dispatch. | A bounded declarative contract is plausible as a meeting point for authoring, discovery output, verification, and explanation. The supported fragment and denotation remain unformalized. |
| Vector-first evidence | Approximate matching requires typed evidence components; meaningfulness requires distinct compression, significance, prediction, stability, novelty, utility, and semantic status; discovery should expose proposal and fit evidence rather than declare meaning. | Evidence profiles should preserve typed components. The two vector models serve different questions and must not be merged into one schema without formal mapping. |
| Matching and discovery are operationally distinct | Discovery literature supports bounded heterogeneous proposal mechanisms; meaningfulness evaluates candidates after selection-aware accounting; complexity results show cheap matching does not imply cheap discovery. | Matching may consume a supplied declarative pattern while discovery searches a bounded hypothesis space. They can share candidate notation without sharing engines, objectives, or guarantees. |
| Compiler-visible computational classification | Relational composition crosses formal classes; discovery requires explicit search bounds; complexity literature separates data, pattern, compiled-state, and search costs. | Each accepted pattern fragment needs a declared computational signature and justified backend. Unknown combinations must not inherit neighboring guarantees. |
| Specialized execution and interpretation | Linguistic analysis requires domain providers and resources; relation types depend on standards or datasets; weighted models and statistical evaluation are task-bound; complexity classes require different algorithms. | Shared semantics, if viable, would sit above specialized providers, matchers, solvers, parsers, statistical evaluators, and search systems. One runtime is not supported. |

### Merely analogous concepts

- A linguistic pronunciation lattice and a discovery hypothesis space both contain alternatives, but the former represents unresolved domain interpretation while the latter represents searched candidate models.
- Approximate-match evidence vectors and meaningfulness evidence profiles are both multidimensional, but one characterizes a candidate relation or match and the other supports claims about a discovered pattern.
- A match witness, a provenance graph, and a statistical evidence record all support explanation, but they answer different questions and have different validation rules.
- Cost-guided discovery and weighted similarity both use numbers, but search priority is not match similarity, scientific evidence, or probability.
- Typed dialects, provider schemas, relational theories, and execution classes all describe modularity; their shared modular shape does not establish one extension mechanism.

These analogies are useful for interface research, not evidence that the underlying concepts should share types or implementations.

### Domain-specific concepts

- Phonemes, graphemes, morphemes, syllables, stress, dialect realization, G2P, pronunciation lexicons, and linguistic feature systems remain specialized linguistic machinery.
- Unicode canonical equivalence, security confusable profiles, OCR models, rendering conditions, and transliteration tables remain relation-provider or standard-specific.
- Affine arithmetic, nonlinear theories, graph topology, parsing semantics, and backreference-like equality retain their own formal models and bounds.
- Null models, perturbation policies, calibration populations, loss functions, utility, causal identification, and semantic grounding remain task- and domain-specific.
- Search proposal algorithms, indexes, learned models, and optimization strategies remain specialized even when they emit a common candidate form.

### Conflicts and unresolved questions

1. **Shared semantic envelope versus one IR.** Several reports benefit from typed observations, relations, and evidence, but none demonstrates that their full graphs, lattices, sequences, statistical records, and execution requirements fit one canonical IR without distortion.
2. **Pattern-language breadth.** Discovery needs an externally defined hypothesis language, while relational algebra deliberately excludes arbitrary computation. It is unresolved whether a useful shared fragment is expressive enough for representative discoveries.
3. **Independent verification.** Reports agree that discovered candidates should be replayed by an ordinary matcher, but independence could mean separate implementation, separate algorithm, or only separate semantic phase. The assurance requirement is not defined.
4. **Ambiguity semantics.** Candidate linguistic analyses, uncertain substitutions, match alternatives, and discovery hypotheses require different commitment and ranking rules. A common alternative type may be too weak or too permissive.
5. **Evidence composition.** Vector-first outputs converge structurally, but no common normalization, correlation model, threshold policy, or cross-task comparison is justified.
6. **Operator interaction.** Relational primitives have plausible local denotations, yet composition with captures, repetition, correspondence, negation, domain projection, and approximate evidence lacks a reviewed decidability and complexity matrix.
7. **Provider trust and reproducibility.** The reports require versioned provenance, but do not settle how nondeterministic, learned, external, or proprietary providers participate in repeatable matching claims.
8. **User and product value.** No evidence establishes a cross-domain user job or advantage over interoperable specialized tools.

### Architectural conclusion

The reports support a narrower hypothesis than a Universal Pattern Engine:

> A bounded shared symbolic framework may be viable if it defines typed observation and projection contracts, declarative relation and pattern semantics, source/span correspondence, witness and evidence interfaces, and compiler-visible computational requirements, while domain interpretation, discovery, evaluation, and execution remain specialized.

This is a research hypothesis, not a selected architecture. The convergence is strongest at contracts and semantic distinctions, weaker at a common declarative language, and absent for a universal IR or runtime.

The most defensible relationship is:

```text
observations + typed projections + explicit context/provenance
                              |
                              v
                 bounded declarative patterns
                              |
                  semantic/computational class
                  /           |             \
       specialized matcher  solver/parser  domain backend
                  \           |             /
                              v
                 witness + typed evidence

bounded discovery --proposes--> declarative candidate
bounded evaluation ---------> evidence profile
ordinary matching ----------> independent replay result
```

The diagram expresses separation of responsibilities, not a component or repository plan.

## Limits, uncertainty, and negative knowledge

Cross-report agreement is partly dependent because reports shared a brief, repository context, and vocabulary. Repetition therefore is not independent evidence by itself. No formal semantics, conformance suite, cross-domain prototype, user study, performance evaluation, security analysis, or downstream review validates the bounded-framework hypothesis. The strongest currently supported conclusion is that certain distinctions should be preserved if research continues—not that STRling should build the system.

## Alternatives considered

One universal IR and runtime; one authoring language with multiple backends; shared observation, result, and provenance contracts only; interoperable specialized DSLs; domain-specific systems with no shared layer; or no generalized product work.

The evidence rejects the first, leaves the second conditional, provides material support for investigating the third, and keeps the fourth and fifth as serious baselines.

## Implications for STRling

The findings may justify a downstream review only if the Program Owner wants to evaluate a narrower bounded framework. They do not justify implementation tasks, changes to the current regex IR, a generalized VM, generic confidence, embedded linguistic machinery, or a discovery engine. Fourth Edition remains independent.

## Recommendations

1. Formalize a minimal cross-inquiry contract for observations, projections, relations, spans, witnesses, evidence types, and computational signatures.
2. Define falsification criteria and compare the contract with interoperable specialized DSLs on at least three structurally different tasks.
3. Require discovered candidates to replay through an independently specified matcher and retain search and evaluation provenance.
4. Audit material primary-source claims before raising any inquiry to Evidence-backed.
5. Ask downstream authority for review only after formalization and prototype evidence, not from this synthesis alone.

## Rejected or abandoned alternatives

The evidence does not support one universal semantic IR, one generalized runtime, one equivalence relation, one confidence scale, one discovery algorithm, or one domain-independent meaningfulness score. The historical UPE roadmap remains abandoned.

## Downstream disposition

**Disposition:** none.

No downstream repository has accepted or implemented this hypothesis. Any architecture or product decision belongs to `strling` and its Program Owner. The [Beyond Patterns program](../../programs/beyond-patterns/README.md) is a research classification only.
