# Negative knowledge

Research is durable when it preserves not only answers but also the boundaries of what is known.

> Unknown is not unsupported.

**Unknown** means the available evidence does not establish the proposition. **Unsupported** means applicable evidence establishes absence or failure in the stated scope. Missing documentation, an unrun probe, an infrastructure failure, and a negative controlled result are different states.

## What inquiries must preserve

Record material:

- unknowns and unresearched scope;
- conflicts between sources or observations;
- assumptions that lack support;
- counterexamples and boundary cases;
- failed or weakened hypotheses;
- rejected and abandoned approaches;
- empirical anomalies and infrastructure failures;
- analytical and methodological limitations; and
- evidence or events that could change the conclusion.

Negative knowledge should be specific enough to guide later work. “More research is needed” is insufficient without naming the missing evidence, unresolved mechanism, population, version, profile, or decision criterion.

## Do not erase failed reasoning

When an approach fails, record what was attempted, why it appeared plausible, what evidence challenged it, and whether the failure is universal or scoped. This prevents repeated dead ends and makes later reconsideration possible when conditions change.

Historical reports may retain obsolete assumptions. Their containing inquiry must identify those assumptions and prevent them from carrying active authority.

## Source conflicts

Preserve each conflicting proposition with source, scope, date, and authority class. Investigate whether versions, APIs, configurations, or terminology reconcile them. If not, mark the conflict unresolved and state what evidence could resolve it.

## Supersession

Materially changing conclusions require explicit supersession. Link predecessor and successor in metadata and prose, explain what changed, and retain the predecessor. A silent rewrite loses the evidence path and can make an abandoned conclusion appear never to have existed.

Routine corrections that do not change the identity of the conclusion may be made in place, with normal version history.

## Review triggers

Each inquiry should identify events that justify review, such as a controlling standard or official documentation change; a relevant runtime, engine, compiler, or target-profile release; new certified conformance evidence; a STRling architecture or product decision; a successful or failed replication; a material counterexample or security incident; or a downstream decision.

Review triggers are not schedules. They identify evidence changes that could alter confidence or applicability.
