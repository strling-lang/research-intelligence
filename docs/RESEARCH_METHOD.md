# Research method

## Research Inquiry model

A Research Inquiry is a durable investigation of a consequential unanswered question. It replaces the older assumption that every question is a representability gap or must produce a feature proposal.

Valid questions include architecture, semantics, correctness, compatibility, portability, evidence, conformance, safety, performance, compiler validation, product design, human factors, adoption, standards, uncertainty, and future capabilities. A representability gap may be relevant, but it is never mandatory.

## Required inquiry content

Every substantive inquiry should cover:

1. **Research question** — the question in terms that allow evidence to bear on it.
2. **Motivation / decision informed** — why the answer matters and which decision it could inform.
3. **Current state** — known implementation, prior research, and live authority at the review date.
4. **Scope** — systems, versions, populations, claims, and time boundaries included.
5. **Non-goals** — nearby work the inquiry deliberately does not attempt.
6. **Research method** — source strategy, experiments, comparisons, formal reasoning, or human research used.
7. **Evidence and source quality** — why the evidence is applicable and how conflicts were handled.
8. **Findings** — what the investigation establishes within scope.
9. **Limits, uncertainty, and negative knowledge** — what remains unknown, disputed, unsupported, anomalous, or method-limited.
10. **Alternatives considered** — serious competing explanations, architectures, or courses of action.
11. **Implications for STRling** — consequences if the findings are accepted, without implying commitment.
12. **Recommendations** — optional, scoped, and separated from findings.
13. **Rejected or abandoned alternatives** — what was dropped and why.
14. **Downstream disposition** — none, accepted, partially accepted, rejected, implemented, or superseded downstream, with evidence.
15. **Sources** — traceable support for material claims.

An exploratory inquiry may say that a section has not yet been researched. Honest incompleteness is preferable to manufactured certainty.

## Workflow

1. **Frame:** identify the decision, stakeholders, authority boundary, review triggers, horizon, scope, non-goals, and initial maturity.
2. **Inventory prior knowledge:** find existing decisions, implementation evidence, research, standards, source code, empirical records, and gaps; separate current authority from history.
3. **Design the method:** choose methods fitted to the claims and state inclusion criteria and limitations.
4. **Gather and verify:** use the strongest applicable evidence; verify versions and provenance; preserve contradictions.
5. **Analyze alternatives:** compare plausible options on explicit criteria and seek counterexamples and disconfirming evidence.
6. **Synthesize:** separate facts, published results, observations, inference, recommendations, hypotheses, and speculation where confusion is possible.
7. **Review:** check applicability, missing authorities, uncertainty, negative knowledge, navigation, and changed downstream status.
8. **Disposition:** send recommendations to the authoritative owner and record its traceable decision without claiming that decision here.
9. **Supersede when necessary:** retain the old inquiry and establish an explicit successor when conclusions materially change.

## Implementation restraint

Research artifacts can demonstrate feasibility or reproduce a result, but they must remain small and inquiry-specific. This repository does not accumulate production compiler passes, product features, conformance datasets, or canonical knowledge tables. A recommendation to prototype does not authorize a prototype in another repository.
