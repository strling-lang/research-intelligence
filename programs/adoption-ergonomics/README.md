# Adoption & Ergonomics

## Purpose

Research maintainability, cognitive cost, developer workflow, embedded tooling, adoption barriers, migration, discoverability, and value for existing regex users.

## Important research questions

- Where does regex remain the clearest and lowest-cost tool, and where do maintenance or portability costs change the decision?
- Which authoring, explanation, migration, and editor workflows reduce real user effort?
- What barriers arise from dependencies, ecosystem fit, trust, performance, familiarity, and interoperability?
- How should benefits be measured without deficit framing or universal developer stereotypes?

## Relationship to STRling

Findings may inform Simply APIs, Semantic STRling, adapters, CLI/LSP/editor work, documentation, and public product positioning. Downstream product and implementation owners decide what to adopt.

## Current evidence boundary

Existing reports identify plausible cognitive, workflow, and adoption issues but rely heavily on secondary synthesis and overbroad rhetoric. They require focused source verification and empirical user research. Regex remains an excellent tool for many simple tasks.

## Future research opportunities

Task-frequency and usability studies, maintenance and comprehension measures, migration and dependency-friction studies, and experiments that distinguish tool adoption from authoring adoption.

## Explicit non-goals

This program does not assume regex users are irrational, prescribe marketing campaigns, declare universal cognitive effects, or own public documentation implementation.
